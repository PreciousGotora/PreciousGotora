# Overview
This project investigates 4-hour Accident & Emergency (A&E) performance across NHS England trusts during 2025–26, with a specific focus on South East trusts. Using Statistical Process Control (SPC), Python, and Power BI, the analysis identifies statistically significant deterioration in performance and distinguishes genuine performance signals from normal operational variation.The project demonstrates how data analytics can support healthcare performance monitoring, operational decision-making, and quality improvement initiatives.

# Research Question
Which NHS trusts in the South East showed statistically significant deterioration in 4-hour A&E performance during 2025–26, and what does SPC analysis reveal about the pattern of deterioration?

# Project Objectives
1. Analyse monthly 4-hour A&E performance across South East NHS trusts.

2. Identify trusts demonstrating statistically significant deterioration.

3. Differentiate common-cause variation from special-cause variation using SPC techniques.

4. Explore the relationship between operational pressure and performance outcomes.

5. Communicate findings through Python visualisations and Power BI dashboards.

6. Generate actionable insights for healthcare operational management.

# Key Findings
## National Performance
Analysis of NHS England A&E performance data showed:

1. Average 4-hour A&E performance of 80.8%

2. Approximately 48 million attendances

3. More than 12 million patients waiting over four hours

4. Approximately 11 million emergency admissions

## Operational Pressure
Performance reduced progressively as operational pressure increased:

1. Low Pressure trusts achieved the highest average performance.

2. Severe Pressure trusts recorded the lowest performance levels.

3. A clear negative relationship was observed between system pressure and A&E performance.

## SPC Insights
SPC analysis highlighted:

1. Sustained downward shifts in performance among selected trusts.

2. Instances of special-cause variation indicating statistically significant deterioration.

3. Performance signals that could not be explained by random variation alone.

4. Increased deterioration associated with periods of severe operational pressure.
